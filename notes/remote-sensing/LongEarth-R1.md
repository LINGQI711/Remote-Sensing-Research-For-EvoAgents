**Venue:** arXiv preprint 2026

**Topic:** Long-horizon temporal Earth-observation reasoning and alignment

## 1. What problem does it address?

Remote-sensing VLMs usually operate on one image, an image pair, or a short sequence. They therefore have limited evidence for reconstructing multi-stage geographic evolution, locating when and where a change occurred, separating persistent change from temporal anomalies, or predicting an unobserved state across a long sequence.

## 2. How does it solve the problem?

LongEarth-Bench defines 12 tasks under four capability groups: evolution summarization, spatial reasoning, anomaly identification, and logical prediction. The benchmark contains about 120k question-answer samples from 117k unique images; sequences average 15.14 frames and extend to 30. A 30k subset adds structured traces that connect key frames and changed regions to the answer. LongEarth first receives supervised fine-tuning with explicit sequence identifiers and structured chain-of-thought traces. LongEarth-R1 then applies GRPO with separate format, temporal, and spatial rewards.

## 3. What experiments were conducted?

- Benchmark data come from SpaceNet 7, SDSU MidWest Flood, DynamicEarthNet, FLAIR#2, and PASTIS-R, covering urban growth, floods, land-surface evolution, cloud or snow interference, and crop phenology.
- Evaluation spans all 12 long-sequence tasks and also checks whether alignment damages performance on standard remote-sensing benchmarks.
- Comparisons cover general and remote-sensing VLMs, including models designed for temporal EO data.
- Ablations isolate explicit sequence identifiers, structured reasoning supervision, and the three GRPO rewards.

## 4. What are the conclusions?

LongEarth-R1 reports the strongest result on each of the 12 benchmark tasks while remaining competitive on standard remote-sensing evaluations. The ablations support the combined use of stable frame references, evidence-oriented supervised traces, and temporal and spatial reward signals. However, the structured traces are initially generated by Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking before automated and human quality control, so the benchmark and training data are not equivalent to fully expert-authored reasoning. This is a trained long-sequence VLM and benchmark, not an autonomous tool-using EO agent.
